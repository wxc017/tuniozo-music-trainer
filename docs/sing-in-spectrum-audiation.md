# Sing in Spectrum — Tonal Audiation

Notes on singing chords in the interval-spectrum solfège and building polyphonic (chord) audiation.

Core idea: you can't phonate a chord with one voice, so the target isn't simultaneous vocalizing — it's **polyphonic audiation**, with the voice as a *probe and anchor*. Fuse the chord into one color (gestalt), sing the anchor, let the rest bloom. Audiate the stack in region-syllables so each voice gets its own consonant tag and the iconic vowel encodes each chord tone's tuning.

## Practice techniques

**Sing inside the chord (resonance is the feeling).** Play the chord on an instrument, then sing each member in turn — third, fifth, seventh — holding it against the sounding chord. What you feel (beating, sympathetic resonance, your voice "locking in") is the bodily sensation of harmony. This is the single best way to install the feeling, because your one voice learns its place in the stack by fusing with it.

**Arpeggiate-and-hold ("bloom").** Sing the notes in sequence, but keep each one ringing in imagination as you add the next. By the last note you're imagining all of them sounding together. This is the standard bridge from sequential access (which the voice can do) to simultaneous audiation (which the ear can hold).

**The drone method (tanpura / Indian-classical style).** Sound a root+fifth drone and sing scale degrees against it. Everything you sing is heard as an interval against the dyad, so you internalize each note's harmonic "feeling" relative to the harmony. Extend the drone to a full triad and you're literally audiating against a chord. This is ancient, proven, and builds exactly the sensation you want.

**Motor imagery — "vocalize without vocalizing."** Imagine the vocal effort of singing each voice — the laryngeal set, the breath — without phonating. Motor imagery recruits much of the same sensorimotor circuitry as real singing (this is why athletes rehearse mentally), and you can cycle through the imagined voices far faster than you can physically sing them, approaching a simultaneous sense. This is the closest thing to your literal request.
