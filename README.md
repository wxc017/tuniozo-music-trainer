# Tunizo

**Warning: AI-assisted program.** I wish I'd written more of this myself, but I have other responsibilities.

A microtonal music practice tool I built for my own daily practice. A few modes I use every day; most are experiments I haven't bothered to delete. I've mainly uploaded this as a backup, since the project is getting pretty big.

It's built around **any EDO** (and JI), an isomorphic **Lumatone** layout, and a consistent **sized-interval** notation — so the same scale, chord, or interval is named the same way in every tuning.

## The ones I actually use

**Ear training (Spatial Audiation)** — the mode I rely on most. Instead of the usual drill-and-response, it plays audio and you play it back on your keyboard. I think drill-and-response is a weak way to train your ear; tying the training to your instrument builds real visualization.

**Tonal Audiation** — intervals, scales, and chords for the current EDO. Scales come from a sized-interval catalog (each mode/family in its Small / Center / Large flavor, with an Exhaustive toggle for the full color palette), and every scale's chords are computed for you — a triad on each degree plus secondary dominants. The voicing options act as a framework laid *into* the chosen scale, so extensions stay in-scale unless you ask for "avoid" tones. Each chord reveals its intervals and solfège (from the tonic), tap to hear any note, syllable, or the whole chord.

**Interval Spectrum** — Margo Schulter's regions of the interval spectrum, drawn as strips. Type EDOs to see where their steps land, ring intervals as drones to compare their color, and read everything in the shared sized notation (small / center / large, neutral, interseptimal, tritone…).

**Drum patterns (Fine Ostinato)** — the other one I use every day. Inspired by Universal Functions, Chaffee patterns, and New Breed concepts. Pick a beat, work through permutations.

**Drum Patterns (Pattern Ostinato / Accent Studies)** — patterns over ostinatos (after one Matt Garstka clinic); accent studies are great fill material.

**Melodic patterns** — Markov chains generate progressions that fit musical melodies, covering most cells you'll run into: generate a progression and improvise melodies over it, generate a melody and reharmonize it, or practice patterns over changes.

**Harmonic lattice** — Monzo notation up to prime 127. Lattices geometrically need higher dimensions, so extra primes show as angles rather than axes.

**Practice log** — day-to-day logs you can import from, so you can pick up where you left off.

## Microtonal scaffolding

- **Sized-interval notation** — one notation everywhere (keyboard labels, scale names, chord symbols): `s/center/l` of each quality, plus neutral, interseptimal, sub/super-fourth, and tritone codes. Minor chords lowercase; non-perfect fifths are signified.
- **Scale catalog** — church modes, neutral, harmonic/melodic minor, harmonic & double-harmonic, pentatonics, symmetric scales, MOS shapes, and curated maqamat — grouped by family and interval limit (Ptolemaic / Septimal / Undecimal).
- **Constructed solfège** that works in any EDO, with audio.
- **Modulation & Borrowing**, **Temperament Explorer**, and a recolored Lumatone keyboard.

## Other modes (varying quality)

Drum accents, piano / Lumatone / guitar views, melodic cells, konnakol drills, transcriptions, chord charts, drill & response, harmony workshop, independence drills, phrase decomposition, vocal percussion.

Some are genuinely useful. Some are nonsense I haven't removed. Take what helps.
